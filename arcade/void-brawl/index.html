What it is

Void Brawl puts you and up to five bots in one arena with a black hole in the middle. Everyone flies the same small ship. Weapons and upgrades drop at random and run out, so you have to move, hide, hunt and grab what you can.

Fire your rope at an asteroid, swing around it to change direction fast, or whip a small rock into a rival. Careless swings hit things and hurt your ship. Pull too close to the black hole and it will have you.

Prefer real rivals? Gather up to five friends on the same Wi-Fi network and fight it out, six ships in one arena, no internet servers needed.

Watch it play

Gameplay: Void Brawl: six ships, one black hole.

Ways to play

  • Classic: every ship has five respawns. The last ship still flying wins.
  • Timed: a four-minute match. Highest score wins.
  • Practice: relaxed play with no pressure, good for learning the rope and the weapons.

Choose how many bots you face, from one to five, and how good they are: Rookie, Pilot, Ace or Legend. The bots have their own styles. Some hunt you, some scavenge, some dance around rocks, some snipe from far away, and some wait to finish off the weak.

Six ships, one Wi-Fi, zero servers

Bots are fun, but nothing beats the moment a friend rope-swings round a rock and slams it into your ship. Void Brawl has local multiplayer: one player hosts, up to five more join, and the arena fills with six real pilots, all in the same room (or the same Wi-Fi).

  • Host: one player starts the match and sets it up.
  • Join: up to five friends on the same Wi-Fi network find the host and hop in. Six ships in all. Every phone needs the same version of the game, because this version can't see older builds in the lobby.
  • Everyone sees who is who: each pilot shows a player name and ship colour, so you know exactly who to blame.
  • Private by design: the game talks directly between your devices on the local network. There is no game server and nothing goes to us. No accounts, no chat.

Your phone may ask for permission to use the local network the first time, so it can find nearby players. Say yes and the lobby fills up. Say no and the rest of the game still works against bots.

Pick-ups, hazards and tricks

  • Weapons: your small blaster never runs dry (but it reloads). Crates give scatter, laser, homing missiles and mines, and vanish after 15 seconds. A laser shows its aim line, and missiles lock on to the nearest ship.
  • Drone: a little helper ship that guards you, then flies off to attack near the end of its life.
  • Rope: grab asteroids, swing around them and sling them. Big rocks are heavy and hard to move. Small rocks are easy.
  • Moves: dash, and a corkscrew roll that leaves a spiral trail.
  • Black hole: pulls harder the closer you fly. Its gravity bends your path from a distance.
  • Void Surge: a wave of particles sweeps the arena every 1:30 and burns any ship in the open. Hide behind a rock, or press SHIELD.

Controls

LEFT THUMBMove your ship.
RIGHT THUMBMove a little to aim. Push out past the outer ring to fire.
DOUBLE-TAP + HOLDOn the right stick: fire your rope.
BUTTONSDash, rope, SHIELD and mines. Move and resize them in Settings. The move stick, aim stick and fire ring each have their own size slider.
SHIELDOrange crates give a shield charge (carry up to 2). SHIELD makes a bubble for 8 seconds that blocks the Void Surge and soaks 40 damage from bullets, missiles, mines and rocks. Lasers count half.

Good to know

  • AccountNone. No chat.
  • PlayersSolo against up to five bots, or up to six players (a host plus five friends) on one Wi-Fi network

The game shows a banner on menu screens and a short ad after some matches, never during a match. If you quit a solo match halfway, an ad may play before your next solo match. In Classic you can choose to watch an ad for one more respawn when you are out. Scores and settings stay on your phone, and Wi-Fi matches stay between the devices in the match.
